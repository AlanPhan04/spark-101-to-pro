\chapter{Further Reading}
\label{app:reading}

Full citations are in the bibliography. This appendix groups them, plus a
few further pointers, by the part of the book they extend.

\section*{Foundations (Part I) and the engine's own papers}

The original research papers behind Spark's execution model are short,
readable, and still the clearest statement of \emph{why} the engine is
shaped the way \Cref{ch:shape} and \Cref{ch:catalyst} describe it:
\emph{Resilient Distributed Datasets} \cite{ZahariaEtAl2012} (the RDD
abstraction and lineage-based fault tolerance underlying every job/stage/task
concept in this book), \emph{Spark SQL: Relational Data Processing in Spark}
\cite{ArmbrustEtAl2015} (Catalyst itself), and the retrospective
\emph{Apache Spark: A Unified Engine for Big Data Processing}
\cite{ZahariaEtAl2016}. \emph{Spark: The Definitive Guide}
\cite{ChambersZaharia2018} and \emph{Learning Spark, 2nd ed.}
\cite{DamjiEtAl2020} cover the same ground at tutorial pace, useful where
this book moves quickly from mechanism to mechanism.

\section*{Making queries fast (Part II)}

\emph{High Performance Spark, 2nd ed.} \cite{KarauEtAl2026} is the closest
comp to this book's own emphasis --- shuffle, joins, and partitioning as
performance levers rather than API surface --- and is the natural next
stop after \Cref{ch:shuffle}--\Cref{ch:caching}. The official
\emph{Tuning Spark} guide \cite{SparkTuningGuide} and
\emph{Spark SQL, DataFrames and Datasets Guide} \cite{SparkSQLGuide}, both
pinned to this book's reference version (\Cref{app:refenv}), are worth
checking directly whenever a config default is suspected to have changed
since.

\section*{The Iceberg lakehouse (Part III)}

The \emph{Lakehouse} paper \cite{ArmbrustEtAl2020} is the argument for why a
table format over object storage exists at all, ahead of Iceberg
specifically. For Iceberg itself: the official
\emph{Apache Iceberg Table Spec} \cite{IcebergSpec} is the primary source
for everything in \Cref{ch:iceberg-spec} on the metadata tree and delete
files, and the \emph{Apache Iceberg Documentation} \cite{IcebergDocs} covers
catalogs, maintenance procedures, and Spark-side configuration in more
day-to-day detail than this book's mechanism-first treatment.
\emph{Apache Iceberg: The Definitive Guide} \cite{ShiranEtAl2024} is the
book-length comp, and the Cazpian engineering blog
(\url{https://cazpian.ai/blog}) was a useful secondary source while drafting
this part --- cross-checked against the official spec and docs above
wherever the two disagreed, most notably on maintenance-operation ordering
(\Cref{sec:ice-write-expiration}).

\section*{Operating Spark (Part IV)}

The \emph{Structured Streaming} paper \cite{ArmbrustEtAl2018} is the
primary source behind \Cref{ch:streaming}'s micro-batch and exactly-once
model. \emph{Apache Kyuubi Documentation} \cite{KyuubiDocs} covers engine
share levels and pooling (\Cref{sec:tuning-kyuubi}) in more depth than a
single chapter section can. \emph{Designing Data-Intensive Applications}
\cite{Kleppmann2017} is not Spark-specific but is the best available
treatment of the distributed-systems ideas this book leans on throughout ---
partitioning, replication, consistency, fault tolerance --- for a reader who
wants the general theory behind a specific mechanism.

\section*{Internals (Part V)}

Most of \Cref{ch:tungsten}--\Cref{ch:dsv2} has no single canonical paper;
the most reliable way to go deeper is the Spark source itself, read
alongside \emph{Spark: The Definitive Guide} \cite{ChambersZaharia2018}'s
higher-level description of the same components and the official
\emph{Spark SQL, DataFrames and Datasets Guide} \cite{SparkSQLGuide} for
whichever configuration surface a given internal exposes:

\begin{description}
\item[Whole-stage codegen] \texttt{sql.execution} package
      (\Cref{ch:tungsten}).
\item[The shuffle framework] \texttt{shuffle} package (\Cref{ch:scheduler}).
\item[DataSource V2] \texttt{sql.connector} package (\Cref{ch:dsv2}).
\end{description}
